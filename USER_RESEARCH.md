# Wearwise — suggested formative usability session

This guide is optional and is not part of the participant-facing app. Suggested tasks are scenarios, not claims about research findings. Ask participants to think aloud; avoid explaining the intended navigation or giving the correct button names in advance. Use the same fictional data and initial state for everyone unless deliberately comparing conditions.

## Before each session

- Confirm the prototype opens on the participant's device and the photo picker works.
- Reset the application (You → Sign out & reset demo) and use the prefilled sample identity; no real email is required.
- Explain that product listings, weather, notifications and purchases are simulated and no payment or external account is required.
- If asking participants to upload a garment, provide the same `assets/hoodie.jpg` file, or obtain consent for their own non-sensitive photo.
- Record task completion, errors, time (if appropriate), participant comments, and assistance needed separately in your research notes. Do not treat these notes as actual results until sessions have been completed.

| Task | Participant scenario | Moderator success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Onboarding | Set the assistant to show cheaper alternatives that suit a casual style, including second-hand items. | Affordability, Style, Second-hand Options on; Casual selected; reaches Shop. |
| 2. Marketplace | Find an affordable knitwear item and see its material, source and price. | Uses filters or category; opens Oatmeal wool jumper and identifies details. |
| 3. Wishlist | Save the organic cotton tee to reconsider later; find it again without searching. | Opens product, saves it, locates it on Saved. |
| 4. Proactive assistance | Explore the shopping-assistant preview. Decide whether to consider the alternative or continue browsing. | Recognises pop-up and its cheaper-price reason; can view in app or dismiss without restriction. |
| 5. No-match behaviour | Change material preferences to wool only, then look at the same preview again. | Understands that no linen notification is shown because the enabled material constraint is not met. |
| 6. Wardrobe reuse | View the “Already own similar” preview and find outfit inspiration from owned items. | Opens Inspo from wardrobe reminder; recognises existing items rather than purchases. |
| 7. Wardrobe upload | Add a clothing photo, name it and assign a category. | Item appears in virtual wardrobe; can view it and find an outfit. |
| 8. Outfit inspiration | Find a casual outfit for university in spring. | Applies filters and can keep/pass an outfit. |
| 9. Notification control | Turn off daily outfit alerts, then inspect the morning preview. | Understands and verifies that the alert disappears. |

## Suggested questions

- What did you expect to happen when you changed a preference?
- Were the shopping-assistant recommendations helpful, disruptive, or neither? Why?
- Did you understand when an item would not be recommended?
- Could you distinguish a second-hand item from a new verified-brand item?
- Did you understand that buying would take you to the original source?
- How easily could you find and style something you already own?
- What information or controls were missing?

## Evaluation mapping

- Norman: discoverability/signifiers (search, filters, hearts, navigation); mapping (switches and notification rules); feedback (saved states, upload and reset); constraints (strict recommendation matching).
- Shneiderman: consistency (persistent navigation and common components), informative feedback and closure (save/upload), user control (dismissal/notification switches), error prevention and reversal (validation, remove/save toggles), reduced memory load (product details and comparison within the pop-up).

These questions and tasks are proposals; adapt them to your approved research methodology and time constraints.
